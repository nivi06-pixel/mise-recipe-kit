Chocolate Chip Cookies

Crisp at the edges, gooey in the middle, with a little flaky salt on top

Vegetarian

Kid-friendly

Freezes well

Why this recipe works. Everyone has a cookie they're chasing: the one from a school bake sale or a grandmother's tin. This one gets there with two small tricks. Melted butter makes the centres chewy instead of cakey, and thirty minutes in the fridge lets the dough turn toffee-sweet and stops it spreading into puddles. Pull them out while they still look a little underdone.

Ingredients

For 24 cookies, in metric.

170 g

unsalted butter, melted and cooled

200 g

light brown sugar

100 g

granulated sugar

1

large egg

1

egg yolk

2 tsp

vanilla extract

280 g

all-purpose flour

1 tsp

baking soda

1 tsp

fine salt

340 g

semisweet chocolate chips

To finish

1 pinch

flaky sea salt, for the tops

Method

1

Whisk the melted butter with both sugars until glossy, about 1 minute. Whisk in the egg, the yolk and the vanilla until smooth and a shade lighter.

Tip: Melted butter gives chewy centres; softened, creamed butter gives cakier cookies.

2

Fold in the flour, baking soda and salt with a spatula until no dry streaks remain, then fold in the chocolate chips.

3

Cover the bowl and chill the dough for 30 minutes, or up to 3 days.

Tip: Resting lets the flour soak up moisture: less spread, deeper toffee flavour.

4

Heat the oven to 180°C (350°F) and line two baking sheets with baking paper. Scoop 2-tablespoon balls of dough, 5 cm (2 in) apart.

5

Bake one sheet at a time for 10 to 12 minutes, until the edges are golden but the centres still look soft. Sprinkle with flaky salt straight away.

6

Leave on the sheet for 5 minutes to set, then move to a rack. Eat at least one warm.

Cook's notes

  • Keeps for 5 days in an airtight tin.
  • Freeze scooped dough balls and bake from frozen: add 2 minutes.
