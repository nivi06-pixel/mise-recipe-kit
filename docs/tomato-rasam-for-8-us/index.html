Tomato Rasam

A peppery, tamarind-sour broth for rainy evenings and slow recoveries

Vegan

Gluten-free

One pot

Prep

10 min

Cook

20 min

Total

35 min

Serves

8 bowls

Print the kitchen cardJump to method

Why this recipe works. Every South Indian home has its own rasam, and every cook swears theirs is the right one. This is the weeknight version: no dal to cook, nothing to soak, just ripe tomatoes, tamarind and a fistful of black pepper, finished with a sizzling tempering. Drink it from a cup, or pour it over hot rice with a spoonful of ghee.

Ingredients

For 8 bowls, in US cups and ounces. Scaled from 4 to 8 bowls. Cooking times stay the same; pans may need to be bigger.

1¾ lb

ripe tomatoes, roughly chopped

1 oz

tamarind pulp, soaked in 120 ml warm water

2 tsp

black peppercorns

2 tsp

cumin seeds

8 cloves

garlic, lightly crushed

1 tsp

ground turmeric

8 1/3 cups

water

1 tbsp

fine salt, or to taste

For the tempering

¼ cup

ghee, or coconut oil to keep it vegan

2 tsp

black mustard seeds

4

dried red chillies, broken in half

4 sprigs

curry leaves

2 pinches

asafoetida (hing)

To finish

1 oz

coriander leaves, chopped, to finish

Method

1

Squeeze the soaked tamarind and strain the liquid into a jug. Throw away the fibres and seeds.

2

Pound the peppercorns, cumin and garlic to a coarse paste in a mortar, or pulse a few times in a spice grinder.

Tip: Coarse is the point: you want little bursts of pepper, not a smooth powder.

3

Put the tomatoes, turmeric, salt and the pepper paste in a pot with the water. Simmer for 10 minutes, mashing the tomatoes against the side of the pot.

4

Pour in the tamarind water and bring it just to a froth. Take it off the heat the moment it rises: boiled rasam turns bitter.

5

Heat the ghee in a small pan. Add the mustard seeds; when they pop, add the chillies, curry leaves and asafoetida. Pour it, sizzling, over the rasam.

6

Cover and leave for 5 minutes so the tempering steeps, then scatter with coriander and serve.

Cook's notes

  • Keeps for 3 days in the fridge and tastes better on day two.
  • For a fuller rasam, stir in 100 g of cooked, mashed toor dal at step 3.
