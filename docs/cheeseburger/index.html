Smash Cheeseburgers

Thin, crusty patties, melted cheddar and a tangy burger sauce on a toasted bun

Weeknight

Crowd-pleaser

Prep

15 min

Cook

10 min

Total

25 min

Serves

4 burgers

Print the kitchen cardJump to method

Why this recipe works. A smash burger is mostly about heat. Press a ball of beef hard into a very hot pan and the whole surface turns brown and crisp in about two minutes, which a thick patty never manages. Two thin patties with cheese melted between them beat one fat one every time, and the sauce takes a minute to stir together.

1 tbsp

1 tsp

yellow mustard

1 tbsp

pickle brine

Method

1

Stir the mayonnaise, ketchup, mustard and pickle brine together for the sauce and set aside.

2

Divide the beef into 8 loose balls of about 75 g. Keep them in the fridge until the pan is ready.

Tip: Handle the meat as little as possible; packed beef cooks up tough.

3

Butter the cut sides of the buns and toast them in a large dry pan or griddle until golden. Set aside.

4

Heat the pan over high heat until it smokes. Add 2 or 3 balls, then smash each one flat and thin with a sturdy spatula. Season with salt and pepper.

Tip: Press hard in the first 30 seconds only. After that, leave them alone.

5

Cook for 2 minutes, until the edges are deep brown and crisp. Scrape them up, flip, lay a slice of cheddar on each and cook 1 minute more. Repeat with the rest.

6

Spread sauce on both halves of each bun, then stack lettuce, two patties, onion and pickles. Eat straight away.

Cook's notes

  • A cast-iron pan or a flat griddle gives the best crust; non-stick won't get hot enough.
  • The sauce keeps for a week in the fridge.
